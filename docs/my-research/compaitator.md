1. 🏫 SchoolAI

SchoolAI

Its fundamental insight

AI should not replace the teacher; AI should make the teacher see and support more students.

SchoolAI’s current product is heavily centered around teacher control + student personalization + visibility.

Its Mission Control shows teachers who is engaged, where students are stuck, their reasoning, class mastery and suggested next steps. Its Student Portal gives students a personal AI tutor while keeping activity visible to teachers. 

It has reached 1M+ classrooms across 80+ countries according to OpenAI/SchoolAI. 

Killer feature

Mission Control

That’s the moat.

The student can be talking to AI, but the teacher isn’t blind.

Business model

B2B / schools / districts

This is powerful because one sale can bring hundreds or thousands of students.

Weakness

It’s fundamentally a school infrastructure product.

That means:

* procurement
* teacher adoption
* district sales
* privacy
* integrations
* school calendars

It’s not designed around:

“I want to have fun studying with my friends tonight.”

SchoolAI’s territory

🏆 Classroom intelligence

⸻

2. 📸 Gauth

Gauth

This is probably the product I’d study most closely if your goal is viral consumer AI education.

Gauth currently has 100M+ Google Play downloads and nearly 2M reviews. Its current product goes far beyond the original photo-math experience: it now offers step-by-step answers, AI live tutoring, voice, whiteboard explanations, a 100M+ question bank and 20M+ tutorial videos. 

Killer feature

Camera → answer

The product starts with a very high-intent moment:

“I don’t understand this question.”

Take a picture.

Boom.

Answer + explanation.

Why this is powerful

It’s incredibly easy to understand.

You don’t need:

* onboarding
* curriculum selection
* teacher
* course setup
* learning plan

You just have a problem.

Snap → solve.

Business model

Freemium → subscription.

Weakness

The fundamental interaction is still:

Student has problem → AI helps solve it.

That’s incredibly useful, but it doesn’t inherently create a social network or daily learning habit.

It can also drift toward:

“AI does my homework.”

That’s a dangerous position educationally.

Gauth’s territory

🏆 Instant academic rescue

⸻

3. 📚 Quizlet

Quizlet

Quizlet is a different beast because its biggest asset isn’t actually AI.

It’s content + study behavior + network effects.

Quizlet says it has 60M learners and 900M+ study sets. 

That’s enormous.

Killer feature

Turn information into something I can study.

Today its AI tools can take notes, slides or PDFs and generate:

* study guides
* flashcards
* practice tests
* summaries
* homework help

It also has a newer conversational experience designed to take students from a question into explanations and then into studying. 

Its real moat

The content graph.

Millions of people have already created study material.

That means a student searching:

“Class 10 biology”

might find an existing ecosystem of materials.

AI makes creation dramatically cheaper.

Weakness

Quizlet can feel like:

a powerful study utility

rather than:

a product I desperately want to open every day.

Duolingo is much better at the latter.

Quizlet’s territory

🏆 Study infrastructure

⸻

4. 🧑‍🏫 Khanmigo

Khan Academy’s Khanmigo is probably the strongest pedagogical competitor.

Its fundamental philosophy is:

Don’t just give the student the answer. Help them think.

Khanmigo works with Khan Academy’s trusted curriculum and also provides teacher tools. Its teacher tools include class snapshots, lesson planning and content creation. 

And its growth has been substantial: Khan Academy reported 2M students, educators and parents used Khanmigo during SY24–25, with signups up 731% year over year. 

Killer feature

Socratic tutoring

Instead of:

“x = 5”

it tries to get you to discover why.

Massive advantage

Trust + curriculum.

Khanmigo isn’t just an LLM wrapper.

It can sit on top of Khan Academy’s enormous educational ecosystem.

Weakness

It isn’t inherently fun.

That’s important.

A student doesn’t necessarily think:

“OMG, I need to open Khanmigo today.”

Compare that with Duolingo.

Khanmigo’s territory

🏆 High-quality AI tutoring

⸻

5. 🦉 Duolingo

Duolingo is the most important company to study if your objective is viral consumer education.

Because Duolingo has cracked something the others haven’t:

Learning as entertainment.

As of Q1 2026, Duolingo reported 137.8M MAUs, 56.5M DAUs and 12.5M paid subscribers. 

That’s an absurd level of engagement for education.

Its genius isn’t AI.

It’s:

XP

↓
streak

↓
league

↓
friends

↓
notifications

↓
competition

↓
return tomorrow

AI then makes the learning experience better.

Duolingo Max currently includes AI features such as Video Call, Explain My Answer and Roleplay. 

Killer feature

The habit loop.

This is what everybody else should be studying.

Duolingo has explicitly said that making the product more social and engaging helped drive user growth and retention. 

Weakness

The model is easiest to apply to subjects that can be broken into small exercises.

Math, science and exam preparation are harder.

That’s exactly where there could be an opportunity.

🏆 My conclusion

If I were building a new company against these five, I’d position it as:

“Duolingo for exam preparation.”

But not by copying Duolingo’s streaks.

I’d make the fundamental unit:

The Challenge

Instead of:

“Complete today’s lesson.”

you get:

🔥 Today’s Challenge

10 questions
5 minutes
1,247 students competing
Can you reach the top 10%?

Then:

AI diagnoses your mistakes.

Then:

AI trains your weak areas.

Then:

you rematch.

That gives you a product with a very clean story:

“Study for your exam by competing against everyone.”

And that is considerably more differentiated than another AI tutor.

The next thing I’d do before writing a single line of code

I’d test whether students actually want the competition.

I would make a tiny MVP around one exam + one subject + one challenge, put it in front of 50–100 students, and measure:

Challenge started → completed → shared → friend joins → rematch

If that loop works, then build the AI personalization.

Because the biggest risk isn’t whether you can build the AI.

You can.

The biggest risk is whether students find the competition itself addictive enough to come back.
